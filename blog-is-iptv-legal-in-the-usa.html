Is IPTV legal in the USA? What the law actually says

Short answer: IPTV itself is legal. Whether a particular IPTV service is legal depends on one thing — whether it has the rights to what it streams.

2026-10-12•6 min read•IPTV Playlist team

The quick answer

Yes, IPTV is legal in the USA as a technology. YouTube TV, Hulu + Live TV, Sling TV, Fubo and Philo all deliver television over the internet — that is IPTV. What is illegal is offering copyrighted channels and films without permission from the rights holders, and since 2020 doing that commercially can be a federal felony.

Search "is IPTV legal in the USA" and you will find two kinds of answers: sellers who say "100% legal" and forums that say "you'll go to jail". Neither is accurate. This guide separates the technology from the licensing, explains the law that changed in 2020, and gives you a practical way to judge any service before you pay.

What IPTV actually is

IPTV — Internet Protocol Television — simply means TV delivered over an internet connection instead of a satellite dish, an antenna or a cable line. A player app reads a list of streams (often an M3U playlist) and shows them as channels. If you want the technical background first, our plain-English guide to IPTV playlists covers it.

Nothing in that process is illegal. A player app, an M3U file and a Fire TV Stick are neutral tools, in the same way a web browser is. The legal question is always about the source: who is sending the streams, and do they have the right to?

Legal IPTV vs. unlicensed IPTV

The line is drawn by licensing. Broadcasters, sports leagues and studios sell distribution rights. A service that has bought (or been granted) those rights is legal; a service that re-transmits channels it never licensed is infringing copyright, however professional its website looks.

The law: the Protecting Lawful Streaming Act (2020)

For years, US criminal law treated illegal streaming more lightly than illegal downloading. Streaming is a "public performance" rather than a "copy", and at the felony level the law only covered copying and distribution. That gap was known as the streaming loophole.

The Protecting Lawful Streaming Act closed it. Signed into law on December 27, 2020 as part of the Consolidated Appropriations Act, it added 18 U.S.C. § 2319C to the federal criminal code. In plain terms, it makes it a felony to willfully run a commercial streaming service that is built for, has no real purpose other than, or is marketed for streaming copyrighted works without authorization. A first conviction can carry up to three years in prison, with higher maximums for aggravating factors and repeat offenses.

Two points matter for ordinary viewers. First, the law targets operators — people running these services for profit. Its sponsors stated explicitly that it was not meant to put mainstream streaming services or their customers at felony risk. Second, it did not change civil copyright law; rights holders can still sue infringers as before.

Is the law actually enforced?

Yes. The clearest recent example is Jetflicks, a Las Vegas–based paid streaming service with tens of thousands of US subscribers. Its operators were convicted in 2024 and, according to the US Department of Justice, sentenced in May 2025 to terms of up to 84 months in prison. The DOJ described it as the first illegal streaming case ever to go to trial, and estimated the value of the infringement at $37.5 million.

Enforcement does not only come from prosecutors. Industry groups such as the Alliance for Creativity and Entertainment regularly take pirate services offline through civil action and cease-and-desist campaigns — which is why unlicensed services so often disappear overnight.

Can a viewer get in trouble for using IPTV?

This is the question most people are really asking, so here is a straight answer:

  • Using licensed IPTV (YouTube TV, Sling, Pluto TV, etc.) carries no legal risk at all.
  • Using an unlicensed service is not what the 2020 felony law was written for, and criminal prosecution of ordinary US viewers has been rare. But "rarely prosecuted" is not the same as "legal". Watching pirated streams is still copyright infringement exposure in principle, and the practical risks — malware, lost payments, sudden shutdowns — are real. We cover them in the real risks of using IPTV.
  • Selling or reselling unlicensed access is where the serious legal exposure sits.

Is a VPN, an M3U file or a Fire Stick legal?

Yes, all three are legal on their own. A VPN is a privacy tool. An M3U file is a text list of links. A Fire TV Stick is a mainstream streaming device. None of them turns an unlicensed stream into a licensed one, and none of them makes a licensed stream illegal. A "fully loaded" stick sold with piracy apps pre-installed is a different matter — that is exactly the kind of device US and European anti-piracy groups target.

How to check whether an IPTV service is legal

Before you pay anyone, run through these questions. A legitimate service will pass most of them easily:

  1. Does it say what it holds rights to? Licensed services name their channel partners. "25,000 channels from every country" for $5 a month is not a licensing model that exists.
  2. Is the app in official app stores? Licensed apps are on Google Play, the Amazon Appstore, Apple's App Store and Smart TV stores.
  3. Is there a real company behind it? Look for a company name, terms of service, a privacy policy and a DMCA or copyright contact.
  4. How do you pay? Card payments through a regulated processor are traceable and disputable. Gift cards and crypto-only payment are red flags.
  5. Is the price realistic? Licensed live-TV packages in the US cost from about $25 to $90+ a month, because the channels themselves are expensive.

In short

IPTV is legal in the USA. Unlicensed IPTV is not, and since 2020 running it commercially can be a felony. As a viewer, the safest route is simple: use services that clearly hold the rights to what they show, install apps only from official stores, and treat "everything for almost nothing" as the warning it is.

Frequently asked questions

Is IPTV legal in the USA?

Yes. IPTV is a legal way to deliver TV over the internet, and services such as YouTube TV, Hulu + Live TV, Sling TV, Fubo and Philo are all IPTV. A service becomes illegal when it streams copyrighted channels or films without permission from the rights holders.

Is it illegal to watch unlicensed IPTV in the US?

The 2020 felony streaming law targets people who operate unlicensed services for profit, and prosecution of ordinary viewers has been rare. Watching pirated streams is still copyright infringement exposure in principle, and it brings practical risks such as malware and lost payments.

What is the Protecting Lawful Streaming Act?

It is a 2020 federal law that added 18 U.S.C. § 2319C, making it a felony to willfully run a commercial streaming service designed or marketed for streaming copyrighted works without authorization.

Are M3U playlists illegal?

No. An M3U file is just a text list of stream addresses. It is legal or illegal only according to the streams it points to.

Is it legal to use a VPN with IPTV?

Using a VPN is legal in the United States. A VPN does not change whether the content you watch is licensed.
